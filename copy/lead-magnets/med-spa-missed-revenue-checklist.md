# Missed Revenue Checklist for Aesthetic Clinics

## Find the booking gaps already sitting inside your client list

Most aesthetic clinics do not lose revenue only because they need more new leads.

They also lose revenue when past clients do not rebook, maintenance windows pass quietly, consults never convert, treatment packages stall, cancellations create empty provider time, and follow-up depends on whoever has time that day.

Use this checklist to quickly spot the easiest missed revenue opportunities in your clinic.

You do not need exact numbers to start. If an item is true or likely true, check it.

---

## Quick Score

For each checked item, give yourself 1 point.

- 0-4 checked: Low visible leakage
- 5-9 checked: Moderate leakage
- 10-15 checked: High leakage
- 16+ checked: Your clinic likely has meaningful recoverable revenue in the existing client base

The goal is not to be perfect. The goal is to see where revenue may already be sitting.

---

## 1. Past Clients Who Have Not Rebooked

Check any that apply:

- [ ] We have clients who came in once and never booked again.
- [ ] We do not regularly review clients who have not visited in 3, 6, 9, or 12 months.
- [ ] We do not have a clear reactivation process for lapsed Botox, filler, laser, facial, skin, or body clients.
- [ ] We rely on the client to remember when they should come back.
- [ ] We do not know how many past clients could reasonably be brought back this month.

Why this matters:

Past clients are usually warmer than brand-new leads. They already know the clinic, the staff, the location, and the service experience. If they have not returned, it is often because follow-up was not timed or structured.

Easy win to look for:

Clients with a previous treatment history and no future appointment scheduled.

---

## 2. Maintenance Follow-Up Gaps

Check any that apply:

- [ ] Botox or tox clients are not followed up with around their ideal maintenance window.
- [ ] Filler clients are not segmented by treatment date, area, or recommended return timing.
- [ ] Laser clients sometimes miss the next session in their treatment plan.
- [ ] Skin or facial clients are not reminded when they are due for their next maintenance visit.
- [ ] Providers recommend follow-up verbally, but the follow-up is not always tracked.

Why this matters:

Many aesthetic treatments are not truly one-time purchases. They depend on timing, maintenance, and consistency. When the clinic does not own the follow-up rhythm, clients drift.

Easy win to look for:

Clients past their ideal return window with no upcoming appointment.

---

## 3. Consults That Never Converted

Check any that apply:

- [ ] We have consultations that did not turn into booked treatments.
- [ ] We do not always follow up after a consult within 24-72 hours.
- [ ] We do not have a second follow-up for consults that went quiet.
- [ ] We do not separate high-intent consults from low-intent consults.
- [ ] We do not track how many consults become booked treatments.

Why this matters:

Consults are expensive opportunities. The client has already shown intent, given time, and discussed a possible outcome. If follow-up is inconsistent, the clinic loses demand it already created.

Easy win to look for:

Consults from the last 30-90 days with no booked treatment.

---

## 4. Package and Treatment Plan Drop-Off

Check any that apply:

- [ ] Some clients start a package but do not complete all sessions.
- [ ] We do not have an automatic reminder when a client is due for the next package visit.
- [ ] We do not track stalled laser, skin, body, or treatment series clients.
- [ ] Clients sometimes leave with a recommended plan but no scheduled next step.
- [ ] The front desk has to manually remember which clients need follow-up.

Why this matters:

Packages and treatment plans often contain revenue that has already been partially sold. The leak happens when the next step is not booked, not when demand is absent.

Easy win to look for:

Clients who purchased or started a multi-session treatment and have no upcoming appointment.

---

## 5. Cancellations and No-Shows

Check any that apply:

- [ ] We have no-shows or late cancellations most weeks.
- [ ] Canceled appointments are not always rebooked quickly.
- [ ] No-show follow-up depends on the front desk having time.
- [ ] We do not track cancellation-to-rebook conversion.
- [ ] We do not know how much provider time is lost to no-shows and late cancellations each month.

Why this matters:

An empty provider slot generates zero revenue. The revenue is not always lost forever, but it becomes harder to recover the longer follow-up is delayed.

Easy win to look for:

Missed or canceled appointments from the last 14-30 days that were never rebooked.

---

## 6. Empty Appointment Slots

Check any that apply:

- [ ] Same-day or next-day openings sometimes go unfilled.
- [ ] We do not have a fast way to notify the right clients when a slot opens.
- [ ] We send openings manually or post them broadly instead of targeting relevant clients.
- [ ] We do not maintain a priority list of clients who would take earlier appointments.
- [ ] We do not match open slots to clients by service interest, provider, or treatment type.

Why this matters:

Last-minute openings are time-sensitive. If the right client does not hear about the opening quickly, the slot disappears.

Easy win to look for:

Clients who recently showed interest, asked for earlier availability, canceled recently, or are due for a related service.

---

## 7. Front Desk Follow-Up Load

Check any that apply:

- [ ] Follow-up depends on memory, notes, spreadsheets, DMs, or manual lists.
- [ ] The front desk is expected to chase inactive clients, consults, cancellations, and no-shows manually.
- [ ] Follow-up quality changes depending on how busy the day is.
- [ ] Providers and front desk do not always have the same view of who needs follow-up.
- [ ] We do not have a clear daily recovery queue.

Why this matters:

Most clinics do not need more manual tasks. They need a clear system that only hands staff the follow-up opportunities that actually need a human touch.

Easy win to look for:

A simple daily list of high-priority clients who are most likely to book if contacted.

---

## 8. Revenue Visibility

Check any that apply:

- [ ] We do not measure recovered bookings from follow-up.
- [ ] We do not track revenue from reactivated clients.
- [ ] We do not know which follow-up messages produce bookings.
- [ ] We do not know which services create the most missed follow-up revenue.
- [ ] We do not have a monthly report showing recovered appointments, refilled slots, and rebooked cancellations.

Why this matters:

If recovered revenue is not measured, the clinic cannot tell whether the problem is list quality, message timing, front desk follow-up, or the offer itself.

Easy win to look for:

Track three simple numbers first: recovered bookings, refilled slots, and revenue from reactivated clients.

## Simple Missed Revenue Estimate

Use conservative numbers.

### Inactive Client Opportunity

1. Estimate how many past clients are likely overdue or inactive:

   Example: 200

2. Estimate your average appointment value:

   Example: $250

3. Use a conservative recovery rate:

   Example: 5%

Formula:

Inactive clients x average appointment value x recovery rate

Example:

200 inactive clients x $250 x 5% = $2,500 in potential recovered appointment revenue

### Empty Slot Opportunity

1. Estimate empty or lost provider slots per month:

   Example: 12

2. Estimate average appointment value:

   Example: $250

3. Estimate how many could realistically be refilled:

   Example: 25%

Formula:

Empty slots x average appointment value x refill rate

Example:

12 empty slots x $250 x 25% = $750 in potential recovered monthly revenue

These are not guarantees. They are a simple way to decide whether the recovery opportunity is worth investigating.

## What To Prioritize First

If most checked items are in:

### Past clients and maintenance

Start with a lapsed client and maintenance follow-up flow.

Best first segment:

Clients past their ideal return window with no future appointment.

### Consults and packages

Start with a consult follow-up or package continuation flow.

Best first segment:

Consults or package clients from the last 30-90 days with no booked next step.

### Cancellations and empty slots

Start with a cancellation rebooking and priority slot alert flow.

Best first segment:

Canceled appointments, waitlist clients, recently interested clients, and clients due for a related service.

### Front desk overload

Start with a recovery queue.

Best first segment:

The top 20-50 highest-priority clients who need follow-up this week.

## The 3 Recovery Questions

Ask these before adding more new lead generation:

1. Which clients already know us but have not booked again?
2. Which clients are due for a treatment, package step, or maintenance visit?
3. Which empty slots could be refilled if the right clients heard about them fast enough?

If the clinic cannot answer these clearly, there is probably missed revenue in the existing client base.
